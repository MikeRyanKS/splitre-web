Most real estate commission software demos show you a clean deal. The useful questions are about the messy ones: the deal that crosses a cap, the agent who joined in June, the two agents on one listing, the plan you changed in March. Twelve questions are below, in roughly the order they tend to matter.

![A demo test deal where the right broker cut is $1,500 of cap room, not the usual $4,500 split](/blog/real-estate-commission-software-questions/questions-that-expose-the-gaps.svg "Ask for this exact deal in the demo. The answer separates real cap tracking from a percentage calculator.")

## What should real estate commission software get right before you pay?

The arithmetic, in the right order, on the awkward deals. Four questions cover it.

1. **Show me a deal that crosses a cap.** Use this example: an agent has paid $16,500 toward an $18,000 cap and closes a deal with $15,000 of gross commission on a 70/30 split. The brokerage should collect $1,500, not $4,500, and the agent should take home $13,500. A tool that answers $4,500 is a percentage calculator with a note field.
2. **Where does a referral come off?** An outside referral and a franchise royalty come off the gross before the split, not out of the agent's share. Ask to see both on one deal.
3. **What is a percentage insurance fee a percentage of?** It should be the agent's own share after the split. Getting this against the gross instead is a quiet, repeating error.
4. **Can the broker share go to two companies, each with its own cap?** Only matters if you split your cut with a partner company. If you do, it is the hardest thing on this list to retrofit later.

Run your own real deal from last month through the demo rather than theirs. If you want a reference answer first, our [free split calculator](/real-estate-commission-split-calculator) will do the single agent version of all four.

## Which questions catch what a demo hides?

The ones about time passing. A demo shows one deal on one day, and almost every expensive surprise is about the second year.

5. **What happens to a confirmed deal when I edit the plan?** The answer you want is nothing. Deals already closed should keep the numbers they were recorded with, permanently.
6. **Can a cap reset on each agent's anniversary instead of January 1?** Some brokerages need it, most do not, and retrofitting it mid-year is unpleasant.
7. **How does an agent who joined in June get their prior cap progress in?** There should be a field for what the brokerage already collected this year, and it should count toward one cap year only.
8. **What does an amendment do to the record?** Voiding the original and linking a replacement keeps an audit trail. Overwriting the old numbers does not.

## What should you ask about getting your data in and out?

Both directions, because one of them is how you leave.

9. **Can I import my roster and my historical deals from a spreadsheet?** Ask what happens to a row the importer cannot match to an agent. Guessing is worse than flagging.
10. **What does the accounting export actually contain?** You want gross commission income, agent net, the brokerage's own revenue, and pass-through amounts separated out. Ask whether it is a file or a live connection: a file your bookkeeper reviews is often the safer answer, and it is what SplitRE does rather than writing to your ledger.
11. **Can an agent get their own statement without calling me?** A per-deal breakdown by email and a year-end summary ends most payout disputes before they start.
12. **What does it not do?** This is the question that tells you the most. Commission software is not a transaction manager, not a compliance document system, and not a CRM. It does not move money or hold trust funds. A vendor who claims all of it is selling you a second migration.

## What should you ask about price?

Ask what the number depends on, not just what it is. Per seat, per transaction, a flat tier by agent count and pay per deal all look similar at ten agents and diverge at thirty. Ask what happens when you deactivate an agent in a slow quarter, and whether there is a setup fee. Our own model is published on the [pricing page](/pricing) rather than quoted on request.
