A company lead commission split gives the brokerage a larger share on business it generated and paid for, most often 50/50 instead of the agent's usual split. The mechanics matter more than the percentage. The deal has to be marked as a company lead when it is entered, and you have to decide whether the brokerage's larger cut counts toward the agent's annual cap.

![A $9,000 commission on a brokerage lead entered at 50/50 and kept off the agent's cap](/blog/company-lead-commission-split/company-lead-off-the-cap.svg "The off-cap setting is what stops a capped agent getting your paid leads for nothing.")

## What is a fair company lead commission split?

One that covers what the lead cost plus your usual margin, which is why 50/50 is so common and why it is not an answer by itself. If you spend real money on portal leads or paid search, the brokerage carried the acquisition cost and the risk that the lead never closes. If the lead came in because your sign is on a corner, that is a thinner argument.

Write down what a closed lead actually costs you before you set the number. Spend for the quarter divided by deals closed from it is rough but honest, and it will usually be larger than you expect. Agents accept a lower split on company business readily enough when they can see that arithmetic. They resent it when the number looks arbitrary.

One thing to decide early: when does a company lead stop being one? Many brokerages move the client to the agent's normal split after a set period, on the basis that the relationship is now theirs. SplitRE has no clock for that. The broker picks the deal type on each deal, so the rule lives in your policy and in whoever types the deal, not in the software.

## Should a company lead count toward the agent's annual cap?

Usually not, and this is the setting that decides whether a lead program survives. [A cap stops the brokerage collecting a split](/blog/real-estate-commission-cap-explained) once it has taken its annual amount from an agent. Leave company leads inside the cap and your best agent gets your paid leads for free from September onwards.

**Example.** A $300,000 sale at 3% is $9,000 of gross commission. The agent is on 70/30 and has already capped, so on a normal deal the brokerage collects nothing and she keeps $9,000 less any post-cap fee. Mark the same deal as a company lead at 50/50 with the type set outside the cap, and the brokerage keeps $4,500 while her cap progress does not move. Before she capped, the same deal would have paid the brokerage $2,700 at her normal split, so the lead type is worth $1,800 extra even early in the year.

The trade-off is real and worth saying out loud to your agents: an off-cap deal means they did the work and got no credit toward capping. Some brokerages split the difference by counting company leads toward the cap at the normal rate and taking the extra share outside it. That is harder to explain, so be sure it is worth the conversation.

## How do you record a company lead so the math happens on its own?

Put it on the plan as a deal type rather than handling it by hand. A plan can offer types the broker picks per deal, such as a company lead at 50/50, and each type can be marked as not counting toward the cap. The picker shows what the type does before you choose it, and deals with no type selected calculate exactly as the plan always did.

Two limits to know. A deal split between agents has no type picker, because co-agents can be on different plans, so a company lead worked by two of your agents needs the shares set by hand. And a deal type is a per-deal choice, so a mis-typed deal is fixed by amending it, not by editing the plan. Editing the plan never reaches back into a confirmed deal.

## Common questions

### Does the agent still pay per-deal fees on a company lead?

That depends on your plan, and it is worth checking rather than assuming. A deal type changes the split or replaces it with a flat fee. Flat charges like insurance and a transaction fee are plan-level, so decide whether a lead deal carrying both a lower split and the usual fees is the deal you meant to offer.

### How does a company lead interact with a team's shared cap?

The same way: if the type is off the cap, it does not draw down the pool. A [team sharing one annual cap](/blog/real-estate-team-shared-commission-cap) makes the setting more visible, because every member feels a pool that stops moving.

### Should we charge a referral fee instead?

Some brokerages do, deducting a percentage off the top before the split rather than changing it. The money lands in roughly the same place. The difference is that an off-the-top fee reduces the gross commission everyone splits, so it quietly reduces the agent's share too.
