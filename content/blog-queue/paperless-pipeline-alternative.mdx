Paperless Pipeline is transaction management software: documents, checklists, deadlines and compliance from contract to close, with commission calculation offered as an add-on module. If what actually hurts at your brokerage is the commission math rather than the paperwork, a commission-first tool is a different kind of answer. Here is how to tell which problem you have.

![Two columns comparing a transaction and compliance tool against a commission and cap tool](/blog/paperless-pipeline-alternative/two-jobs-one-brokerage.svg "Most brokerages feel one of these two pains long before the other.")

## What does Paperless Pipeline do?

Its own product pages describe a transaction system first: tracking documents, deadlines and compliance, flagging missing required documents, multi office support, an agent portal, an audit trail of files and approvals, and e-signatures sent from inside a transaction. That is the core.

Commissions are a separate module you add on. The pages for it say it calculates splits, caps, fees, referrals, mentor splits and team overrides, warns you before an agent crosses a threshold or a cap, generates the CDA for the closing table, and produces agent statements with year to date totals and around a dozen downloadable reports. They also say it does not generate or file 1099-NEC forms, and that a closed transaction can create a bill in QuickBooks through Zapier.

So this is not a tool with a commission gap. The question is whether you want to buy a transaction system to get at the commission engine attached to it.

## When is a Paperless Pipeline alternative the right call?

When your documents are already handled and your commissions are not. Plenty of independent offices run compliance in SkySlope or Dotloop, or in a shared drive with a checklist, and are perfectly happy with it. What breaks is the spreadsheet where the splits live.

Two other signs. One is that nobody at the office wants a second compliance workflow to learn, because the one you have is the one your agents already use. The other is that your plan rules are the complicated part: an agent whose cap resets on their hire anniversary, a royalty that stops at its own yearly figure, a team sharing one pool, two companies dividing your cut. That is where the money quietly leaks, and it has nothing to do with whether the lead based paint disclosure got uploaded.

## What should a commission-first tool handle?

Judge any of them on the awkward deals, not the simple ones. Anyone can do 70/30.

- The deal that crosses the cap mid transaction, split correctly rather than rounded off
- Caps that reset on each agent's anniversary instead of January 1
- A fee with its own yearly ceiling, tracked separately from the split cap
- Two agents on one transaction, each running through their own plan and cap
- An export your bookkeeper can post from without retyping anything

**Example.** An agent on 80/20 has paid $15,100 toward a $16,000 cap, so $900 of room is left. A deal closes with $9,000 of commission. The full 20% would be $1,800, which is more than the room. The first $4,500 of the commission is split at 80/20, giving the brokerage its last $900 and the agent $3,600. The remaining $4,500 is all the agent's, so the agent takes $8,100 and the brokerage takes $900. Miss the crossing and you have overcharged a producing agent by $900 on one deal.

Real things, and you should know them before you switch. SplitRE does not store transaction documents, run a compliance checklist, chase deadlines or send e-signatures. It does not produce the CDA form. It does not file anything, and it does not move money: it calculates and records.

It also has no live accounting connection. The accounting handoff is a CSV export with invoice and bill descriptions already written, not a sync. Some brokers prefer that, because nothing posts to the books without a person looking at it. If you want a closed deal to create the bill by itself, you want a tool built that way.

## Common questions

### Can we run both?

Yes, and a lot of offices should. Compliance in one tool and commissions in another is a normal setup, and it is usually cheaper than buying a suite for one module.

### Will we lose our history if we move the commission side?

Not if you import it. Past deals come in from a CSV with their closing dates, and you can carry each agent's cap progress forward so the current year is not reset to zero.
