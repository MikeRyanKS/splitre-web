A desk fee plan charges the agent a fixed amount every month and keeps a small percentage of each deal. A commission split charges nothing until a deal closes, then takes a larger percentage, usually up to a yearly cap. Desk fees favor agents who close steadily. Splits favor new and part-time agents. Which one suits your brokerage depends on how many deals your typical agent closes.

## Desk fee vs commission split: how do they differ in practice?

A split is paid out of each closing. Say 70/30 with an $18,000 [commission cap](/blog/real-estate-commission-cap-explained): the brokerage takes 30% of each deal until it has collected $18,000 from that agent in the year, and the agent keeps everything after that.

A desk fee is paid on a calendar, whether or not the agent closes anything. Because the fee covers part of your overhead, the percentage on each deal can be much lower. Many offices combine the two and add a flat per-transaction fee, which keeps applying even after a cap is reached.

## What does each plan collect from agents of different production?

Example: three plans, the same three agents, average commission of $9,000 a deal. E&O and franchise fees are left out so the plans compare cleanly.

| Agent | 70/30, $18,000 cap | 90/10 plus $400 a month | 80/20, $12,000 cap, $250 a deal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 deal a year | $2,700 | $5,700 | $2,050 |
| 4 deals a year | $10,800 | $8,400 | $8,200 |
| 12 deals a year | $18,000 | $15,600 | $15,000 |

The break-even between the first two plans is $24,000 of gross commission, a little under three deals. Below that the split is cheaper for the agent. Above it the desk fee wins.

Look at it from the agent's chair. The one-deal agent earns $9,000 and hands $5,700 of it to you on the desk fee plan. She keeps $3,300. She will either leave or never sign. The twelve-deal agent keeps $92,400 on the desk fee plan against $90,000 on the capped split, and will notice.

## Which plan fits which brokerage?

Ask three questions before you publish a plan.

- **How many of your agents are full time?** A bench of part-timers will not accept a monthly charge. A bench of producers will prefer it.
- **How much of your overhead is fixed?** Rent and staff arrive monthly whatever your agents close. A desk fee matches that cost to the same rhythm.
- **Do you want to recruit producers?** Experienced agents compare total annual cost across offices, not the headline split. A cap or a low desk-fee percentage is what they look for.

A hybrid is the usual compromise: a moderate split, a modest cap, and a flat per-transaction fee.

## Common questions

### Can a desk fee plan also have a cap?

Yes. Put a cap on the percentage you take from each deal and keep the monthly fee outside it. The monthly fee never counts toward the cap, because it is not collected from a deal.

### Do E&O and franchise fees change the comparison?

They change the totals, not the shape. Both come out of each deal whichever plan you pick, so add them to the figures above before you hand an agent a number.
