Explain the split as two separate things: the percentage you keep, and the per-deal fees that come out afterward. Agents hear one number at recruiting and expect to see it on every check. Walk a real closing through with them, in order, and then send the same breakdown after every deal so the next question answers itself before it reaches you.

## How do you explain commission splits to new agents?

Start with the order of operations, because that is where the misunderstanding lives. Anything paid off the top, such as a franchise royalty or an outside referral, comes out of the gross first. Your split is calculated on what is left, not on the whole commission. Then the agent's own fees come out of their side.

Use one of their likely deals rather than a round $10,000 example. A new agent who sees their actual price point run through the plan asks better questions, and you find out in the first week whether they understood the cap. Our [step by step commission calculation](/blog/how-to-calculate-real-estate-agent-commission) is a useful handout for exactly this conversation.

## Which payout numbers cause the most questions?

Three, in roughly this order: the deal that crosses the cap, the fee an agent forgot about, and the month an agent was on a co-agent deal and saw someone else's figures.

**Example.** A $350,000 sale at 2.5% produces $8,750. Kate is on 70/30 against an $18,000 cap, and your brokerage has already collected $15,900 from her this year. A straight 30% would be $2,625, but only $2,100 of cap room is left, so that is all you take. Her share is $6,650, less $150 of E and O and a $395 transaction fee, which leaves $6,105. That is $525 more than the same deal would have paid her a month earlier, and she will ask why. The answer is that she capped on this transaction, which is the good news she hired you for.

## What should an agent see without having to ask you?

A statement per closing, their cap position, and a year-end total. SplitRE emails the agent their full breakdown when you confirm the deal, with the deal number in the subject line so a later question can point at one transaction. You can turn those emails off per agent or for the whole brokerage, though turning them off mostly moves the work back to you.

Two more pieces help. Every deal has a print and share page carrying the same itemized breakdown, which is what you send when an agent wants a copy months later. And each agent has an annual earnings summary PDF covering their confirmed deals for a calendar year, useful for their own records, though it is not a tax document and should not be handed over as one.

Be straight about one limit: on a deal split between two agents, everyone on it receives the whole breakdown. There is no private version, because co-agents can work out each other's take-home from the numbers anyway.

## Common questions

### Should agents see what the brokerage keeps?

On their own deals, yes. Hiding the broker cut on a statement invites the assumption that it is larger than it is, and an agent comparing offers will reconstruct it from their payout in about a minute. What you do not owe anyone is a roster-wide view of other agents' plans.

### What if a payout turns out to be wrong?

Amend the deal rather than quietly editing it. An amendment keeps the original and records what changed as a before and after, and the agent's email leads with a plain summary of it, so the correction is documented instead of disputed.
