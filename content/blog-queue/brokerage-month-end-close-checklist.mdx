A real estate brokerage month end close has one job: get every commission that closed last month recorded, agreed and in the accounting file before anyone asks about it. Five steps do most of the work. Confirm the deals, check the closing dates, read the cap report, export the file, then reconcile what was actually paid out.

![Five month end steps in order, from confirming drafts to reconciling payouts against the bank](/blog/brokerage-month-end-close-checklist/month-end-close-in-five-steps.svg "The order is deliberate. A cap report read before the drafts are confirmed is a report of the wrong month.")

## What belongs in a real estate brokerage month-end close?

Start with the deals that closed but were never finished. A draft deal is not a financial record: it has no deal number, no frozen breakdown, and it adds nothing to an agent's cap progress. If a sale closed on the 27th and the broker saved a draft and went home, that commission is missing from every report you are about to run. Confirming it is the first step for a reason.

Then check the closing dates. The closing date, not the day someone typed the deal in, decides which month and which cap year a deal belongs to. A broker catching up in early January will be confirming December closings, and those belong in December. This is the single correction that most often changes a month's totals after you thought the month was done.

Third, read the cap report before the export rather than after. The [Agent Cap Report](/docs/exporting-your-brokerage-data) lists every confirmed deal per agent and cap year with the amount that counted toward the cap, the running total, and what is left. An agent who is $900 from their cap matters to next month's forecast, and an agent who crossed it last month is the first person who will query their statement.

## Which numbers do you reconcile, and against what?

Three figures per month, and they do not add up the way people expect. Gross commission income is what the brokerage invoiced. Agent net is what it owes the agents. Broker net is the brokerage's own revenue, which is the split plus brokerage fees and nothing else.

**Example.** A six deal month. Gross commission income is $84,000. Agent payouts total $57,400. Broker net is $21,600. The difference is a $5,000 franchise royalty that passed straight through to the franchisor. Gross minus agent net is $26,600, which is not the revenue figure, and a bookkeeper who records it as revenue has overstated the month by $5,000. Pass-through costs belong on their own line as a bill, the same as an outside referral would.

Reconcile the agent payout total against what actually left the account. The commission check arrives as one amount, so the deposit should match gross commission income for the deals that funded, and the payouts should match agent net for the deals that were paid. [Recording both sides in the accounting file](/blog/quickbooks-online-real-estate-brokerage) walks through the invoice and bill pairing.

## What goes into the accounting file?

One row per deal, and on a co-agent deal one row per agent. The export carries the close date, the property, the agent, the sale price and commission percentage, then gross commission income, agent net and broker net, plus pre-formatted invoice and bill descriptions. Where a deal paid another company, a franchise or a partner brokerage, that amount gets its own column so you can enter it as a bill instead of burying it in revenue.

It is a CSV file you import or key in. There is no live connection to your accounting system, nothing writes to the ledger on its own, and no one needs a login to your books. For a small brokerage that is usually the right trade: one file a month, reviewed by a person, and no sync to debug at the worst possible moment.

Two things this close does not cover. Trust and escrow account reconciliation is a separate exercise with rules that vary by state, so confirm the requirements with the state real estate commission and with the broker's CPA. Agent billing is also outside it: a desk fee that comes off a commission is handled in the deal, but recurring invoicing of agents is not.

## Common questions

### What do I do about a deal that was amended?

An amendment voids the original and creates a linked replacement, so a month can hold both versions. Export confirmed deals only and the voided original drops out. The audit history shows which fields changed and what the payout became.
