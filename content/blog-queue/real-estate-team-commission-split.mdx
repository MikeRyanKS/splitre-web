A real estate team commission split has two layers. Each member's deal still runs through their own commission plan, so the brokerage takes its usual cut first. On top of that, the team lead can earn a set percentage of each member's commission. The team also shares one annual cap, so every member stops paying a split at the same moment.

![A $12,600 deal showing a lead override taken off the member's share and off the full commission](/blog/real-estate-team-commission-split/override-two-bases-one-deal.svg "The member nets the same figure under both settings. What changes is how the lead and the brokerage divide the rest.")

## How does a real estate team commission split work?

The member's own plan runs first, exactly as it would on a solo deal. Say Dana is on 70/30 and her team shares a $24,000 cap. A $420,000 sale at 3% produces $12,600 of gross commission, the brokerage takes $3,780, and that $3,780 draws down the team's pool instead of Dana's own cap. When the pool reaches $24,000, every member flips to a 100% split on the same day, whichever of them got the team there. The mechanics of the pool are covered in our guide to [running a team on one shared cap](/blog/real-estate-team-shared-commission-cap).

A lead is optional. Leave that field empty and the team is nothing more than a shared pool: same plans, same fees, one cap instead of several.

## Who pays the team lead override?

By default the member pays it, out of their side of the deal. It is income from one agent to another, so it is not brokerage revenue and it never draws down the cap. Two rules keep it sane. A lead's own deals never pay an override to themselves. And on any single deal you can switch the override off, which is what you want for a member's sphere-of-influence listing the lead had nothing to do with.

**Example.** Dana's $12,600 deal, 70/30, with a 25% override to her lead. The brokerage takes $3,780. Dana's share is $8,820. The override is 25% of that, so $2,205 goes to the lead and Dana keeps $6,615 before her own per-deal fees. Set up this way, the override costs the brokerage nothing on this deal. Your 30% is calculated before the lead takes anything.

## Should the override come off the member's share or the whole commission?

Pick based on who you want to fund the lead, because the member takes home the same amount either way. Off the member's share, the override is a slice of what the agent kept after the split, and your cut is untouched. Off the full commission, it comes out before the split, which shrinks the amount your 30% is calculated on. On Dana's deal that is the difference between keeping $3,780 and keeping $2,835, with the lead earning $3,150 instead of $2,205.

The second setting is the lever you have when you are recruiting a team in: the brokerage funds part of the override rather than the member funding all of it. It is a real cost, so model it against a full year of that team's volume before you agree to it. ## Common questions

### Does the override show up anywhere the lead can check?

Yes. A team lead's annual earnings statement includes the override income earned from members for that year, alongside their own deals. SplitRE calculates and records the amount; it does not pay anyone, so the transfer itself is still your payout run.

### What happens to a member's cap when they leave the team?

You choose where their individual tracking resumes: from zero, from what they personally contributed to the pool this year, or a figure you agreed with them. Deals already closed under the team keep their recorded numbers. The full setup is in the [teams documentation](/docs/teams).
