Commission software is billed on one of four things: your active agent count, a flat price per agent-count tier, each transaction processed, or each deal you confirm. Two quotes built on different units cannot be compared by looking at the monthly figure, and the one that looks cheaper in March is often the expensive one by August. The unit decides, not the number.

![Four shapes a commission software bill takes, from per active agent to pay per confirmed deal](/blog/commission-software-pricing-models/four-shapes-of-a-software-bill.svg "Only the last two shapes move with how busy you actually were.")

## Which pricing models does commission software actually use?

Four, and they behave very differently in a slow quarter. Per active agent charges for every name on the roster whether they closed anything or not. A flat price per agent-count tier is a step function: you pay the same inside a band, then jump when you cross it. Per transaction charges for each deal the system handles. Pay per deal does the same with no monthly floor underneath it.

Some vendors publish a price list. Others quote per brokerage, which means you will not know your number until a call, and it means the published comparisons you can find are guesses. ## How do you compare a per agent price with a per deal price?

Divide, then check against last year. The break-even is the annual flat figure divided by the per deal figure, and the answer is a number of deals a year. Below it the per deal quote is cheaper. Above it the flat quote is.

![The break-even calculation expressed as deals a year](/blog/commission-software-pricing-models/break-even-in-deals-a-year.svg "Two quotes, one number, and it is your deal count that decides which side of it you are on.")

Use your confirmed deal count from last year, not the number you are hoping for. Then do it twice more, once for a bad year and once for a good one, because the point of the exercise is to find out which quote punishes you for growing. A per-agent model punishes you for recruiting. A per-deal model punishes you for a record year, which is the better problem to have.

Watch the agent count definition too. Ask whether an inactive agent still counts, what happens to someone who leaves in February, and whether the count is taken monthly or at signup. Those three answers can move a per-agent bill by a third without anybody quoting you a different price.

## Which model fits a brokerage with a lumpy year?

The one that bills nothing in a quiet month. Most independent brokerages have a real season, and a seat-based bill arrives identical in February and July while the commission income behind it does not.

That is the argument for per-deal pricing at small volumes, and it stops being the argument somewhere around the break-even you just calculated. A brokerage closing a steady twenty a month has no use for it: a flat tier is less arithmetic and usually less money. The honest summary is that per-deal suits low or unpredictable volume and flat tiers suit predictable volume, and your own count tells you which you are.

## What is not in the quoted number?

Setup and migration, minimum terms, and the cost of the thing going wrong. Ask about a setup or onboarding fee, a seat minimum below your roster, whether the term is annual, and what historical data import costs if it is not free.

Then weigh all of it against one mis-split deal.

**Example.** An agent with $1,500 of cap room left closes a $15,000 commission. The correct broker cut is the $1,500 that remains, not the usual 30%, so a system that takes the full $4,500 overcharges her by $3,000. ## Common questions

### Is per transaction the same as pay per deal?

Not quite. Per transaction usually sits on top of a platform fee, so you pay both. Pay per deal generally means there is no monthly figure at all, and the bill is zero in a month with no closings. Ask which one you are being quoted.

### Should we take the annual discount?

Only if you are confident about the next twelve months and have seen the product handle your actual plans. An annual term on software you have used for a week is a bet, and migrating off it mid-term costs you the remainder.

### Does a cheaper tool mean less accurate math?

No, and the reverse is not true either. Price tracks the size of the company selling it and how many other things it does, not the quality of its arithmetic.
