A mentor mentee commission split pays an experienced agent for coaching a new one through their first few closings. It is usually a percentage of the mentee's commission on a set number of deals, paid out of the mentee's side rather than the brokerage's. You can record it two ways, and the choice changes what your brokerage collects on the same transaction.

![A $9,000 commission split as a mentor fee on the plan and as a co-agent deal](/blog/mentor-mentee-commission-split/mentor-fee-versus-co-agent-share.svg "Both routes pay the mentor and leave the mentee with a working deal. The brokerage's own cut is what moves.")

## How does a mentor mentee commission split usually work?

The written agreement names a percentage and a stopping point. The percentage varies a great deal between brokerages, and so does the stopping point: a fixed number of transactions, usually the mentee's first few, or a dollar ceiling, or both. Programs at the franchise brands differ office by office, so treat any figure you come across as somebody else's agreement rather than a benchmark.

The part brokers get wrong is the base. A mentor fee charged on the gross commission is a far bigger number than the same percentage charged on the mentee's share after your split, and new agents rarely notice the difference until the first check lands.

## Should the mentor's cut be a plan fee or a share of the deal?

A plan fee is the simpler of the two. In SplitRE it is a Mentor / Training Fee: a percentage of the agent's own share after the broker split, charged on however many of her opening deals you set, and named to the mentor so it leaves your revenue figures and lands in the export as money paid out rather than income you kept.

A co-agent deal is the other route. The mentor goes on the transaction with a share of the commission, and each agent's share then runs through their own plan, their own fees and their own cap. Use it when the mentor genuinely worked the deal.

**Example.** A $300,000 sale at 3% is $9,000 of gross commission. The mentee is on a 50/50 plan.

- **As a plan fee.** Your brokerage takes $4,500 and the mentee's share is $4,500. A 20% mentor fee on that share is $900, so the mentee keeps $3,600 before her own per-deal fees.
- **As a co-agent deal** with 25% to the mentor: the mentee's slice is $6,750 and the mentor's is $2,250. The mentee's 50/50 sends $3,375 to the brokerage. The mentor, on an 80/20 plan, sends $450. Your brokerage collects $3,825 instead of $4,500, the mentor keeps $1,800 instead of $900, and the mentee keeps $3,375.

![A commission plan being set up with split, cap and fee rules](/screenshots/splitre-commission-plan-setup.png "A mentor fee is a percentage of the agent's share, so it is unaffected by any other per-deal fee on the same plan.")

## What does each route do to the cap and the books?

The plan fee leaves the whole $4,500 against the mentee's cap, which moves a new agent toward capping faster than her production alone suggests. The co-agent route splits the cap contribution between two people, so each one's progress reflects what they personally earned. Deal types are not available on a deal split between agents, since the two agents can be on different plans, so a company-lead rate and a mentor share cannot be combined on one transaction.

Neither route moves money. SplitRE calculates the mentor's amount and records it on the deal, and your payout run still writes the checks. How the mentor's income is reported at year end depends on how you have papered the arrangement, so put that question to your CPA before the first mentee closing rather than after it. ## Common questions

### Does the mentor fee apply to every deal the mentee closes?

No. You set a number of deals and it charges on that many in the cap year, then stops. It also stops early if the mentee caps before she gets there. The count runs in the order deals are confirmed rather than the order they closed, so confirming a backdated closing late does not send the counter back to re-rank the others.

### Can the mentor be on a deal without being a mentor on the plan?

Yes, and that is the co-agent route. Adding a second agent to the deal gives each a share of the commission, the same way a [co-listing between two of your agents](/blog/co-listing-commission-split) works.
