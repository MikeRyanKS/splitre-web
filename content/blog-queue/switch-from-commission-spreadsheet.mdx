Switching from a commission spreadsheet to software takes an afternoon for most independent offices, as long as you do it in the right order: plans first, then agents, then what each agent has already paid toward their cap this year, then the closed deals you want on the record. Get the third step wrong and every cap starts over, which agents notice immediately.

![Four ordered migration steps: plans, agents, cap amounts already paid, then closed deals](/blog/switch-from-commission-spreadsheet/migration-order-four-steps.svg "Step three is the one that goes missing, and it is the one your best producers will catch.")

## What do you need before you start?

Three things, all of which you already have somewhere.

A written version of each commission plan you actually use. Not the one in the recruiting deck, the one you apply. Split, cap amount, when the cap resets, every fee, and who pays it. Most small offices turn out to have two or three plans plus a handful of per agent exceptions.

A roster with names and email addresses. Email is what matching runs on later, so it is worth fixing the two agents who share a surname now rather than during an import.

A list of this year's closed deals: closing date, address, sale price, commission percentage, agent. If your spreadsheet has more columns than that, good, but those five carry the weight.

## How do you switch from a commission spreadsheet to software?

Build the plans first, because agents attach to them and deals attach to agents. A plan with the wrong cap figure quietly mis-prices every deal that follows it, and fixing it afterwards does not reach back into deals you have already confirmed.

Then import the roster. Agents come in from a CSV and each one picks up the default plan, so you only touch the exceptions afterwards: the agent on 75/25, the one with their own cap figure, the two on a team.

Then set each agent's cap opening balance, meaning the amount you have already collected from them this cap year before the software existed. This is the step people skip.

**Example.** An agent is on 70/30 with a $16,000 cap and you have already collected $14,500 from them this year. Their next closing earns $15,000 of commission. With the opening balance entered, only $1,500 of cap room is left, so the first $5,000 of the commission is split 70/30 and the rest is all theirs: $13,500 to the agent, $1,500 to you. With the balance left at zero, the software takes a full 30% and hands you $4,500. That is a $3,000 overcharge on one deal, to the agent most likely to leave over it.

Deals come last. Import this year's closings as confirmed so cap progress and reporting are right, and leave older years out unless you want them for reference.

## What happens to the deals you already closed this year?

You have two honest options and they both work.

Import them, and your dashboard, cap bars and year end totals are complete from day one. The import freezes each deal's breakdown exactly as it comes in, so those figures stay put even if you edit the plan next month.

Or skip them and carry the cap forward with the opening balance instead. The caps will be right, the year to date reporting will not, and you will be running two records until January. That is fine for an office switching in November and a poor idea for one switching in February.

Pick one per agent, not a mixture. An agent who has both imported deals and an opening balance covering the same closings is counted twice, and their cap finishes early.

## What should you check before you trust it?

Re-run three deals you already paid out and compare the agent net to what you actually wrote the check for. Choose the three most awkward ones: a cap crossing, a deal with a referral, a co-listing. If those three match, the straightforward ones will. If one does not, the plan is wrong, not the arithmetic.

Check the cap bars next. Every agent's progress should equal their opening balance plus what the imported deals contributed. Then export a month to CSV and hand it to your bookkeeper before month end rather than during it.

Keep the spreadsheet. Not as a working document, as a read only copy of what the numbers were on the day you moved. It costs nothing and it settles any argument about the handover in about a minute.

## Common questions

### How long does this really take?

An afternoon for a ten agent roster with two or three plans. Longer if every agent has a bespoke arrangement, which is itself worth knowing, since that is the thing making your month end slow.

### What if an agent's name does not match?

Matching runs on email first, then an exact name. A row that cannot be tied to one agent with confidence is flagged for you to choose rather than guessed at. The [CSV import guide](/docs/importing-agents-and-deals-via-csv) covers the column format and what each warning means.

### Do we have to stop entering deals while we move?

No. Build the plans and the roster, enter new deals as they close, and backfill the history when you have an hour. The [commission split calculator](/real-estate-commission-split-calculator) covers you for a one off in the meantime.
