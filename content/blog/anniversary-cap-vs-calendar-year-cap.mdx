A calendar-year cap resets for every agent on January 1. An anniversary year commission cap resets on the date each agent joined your brokerage, so every agent has their own twelve-month cap year. The choice matters most for agents who join mid-year. On a calendar cap they get a short first year and then a second cap right away. On an anniversary cap they get one full year.

The anniversary cap collects once.")

## How does an anniversary year commission cap differ from a calendar cap?

Under a calendar-year cap the brokerage counts what it has collected from an agent from January 1 to December 31. At the stroke of midnight the counter returns to zero for everyone. It is simple to explain and simple to audit, and it lines up with your tax year.

Under an anniversary cap each agent has their own counter. An agent who joined on June 1 is capped from June 1 to May 31, then starts again. Some franchise brands reset caps this way, which is why agents who move between offices ask about it.

Plan documents differ, so read the signed agreement before you copy anyone's numbers. For the basics of how a cap works, see [the commission cap explained](/blog/real-estate-commission-cap-explained).

## How does a June hire change the numbers?

Example: an agent joins on June 1 on an 80/20 split with a $12,000 cap. She closes deals worth $10,000 in gross commission every month, so the brokerage earns $2,000 a month until the cap.

- **Anniversary cap.** She pays $2,000 a month for six months, hits the $12,000 cap in November, and keeps 100% of her commission from December through May. The brokerage collects $12,000 in her first twelve months.
- **Calendar cap.** She caps in November. The counter resets on January 1, and she pays $2,000 a month again from January through May, another $10,000. The brokerage collects $22,000 in the same twelve months.

That is $10,000 more from the same agent doing the same work, which is why calendar caps are attractive to a brokerage and why an experienced agent weighing two offers will ask about the reset date. If your cap is part of how you recruit, an anniversary reset is easier to sell.

## Which should your brokerage choose?

Choose the calendar year when you value simplicity. Everyone's year-end lines up, your bookkeeper reports one cap year, and you rarely recruit mid-year.

Choose anniversary caps when you hire all year, compete with franchises that use them, or want a new agent's first twelve months to feel fair.

The two can also be mixed. An agent with no anniversary date on file keeps resetting on January 1, so you can move to anniversary caps without forcing everyone onto them on day one.

## How does SplitRE handle each one?

By default every cap resets on January 1. To use anniversary caps, set "Annual caps reset" on the Commission Plans page to the agent's anniversary date, then enter each agent's date in their drawer. A deal's closing date, not the day you confirm it, decides which cap year it counts toward. That means a backlog of December deals confirmed in January still lands in the right year. The details are in [Setting Up Agent Caps](/docs/setting-up-agent-caps).

One trade-off: the Annual Earnings Summary PDF stays a calendar-year statement. In anniversary mode it reports what was collected on that year's deals rather than an "x of cap" figure.

## Common questions

### What happens when a new agent already paid part of their cap elsewhere?

Enter that amount as a cap opening balance on the agent. It counts toward one cap year only, so it does not follow the agent into the next one.

### Can I switch from calendar to anniversary caps later?

Yes. Past confirmed deals keep the numbers they were recorded with, so the change only affects deals from then on. To see a cap crossing on one deal, run it through the [free commission split calculator](/real-estate-commission-split-calculator).
